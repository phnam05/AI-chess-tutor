What changed since round 1 (from your answers)

  • “Opens a line” is only said when it matters: in the opening, when the piece gets a way out (a square off its home rank); later, only if the piece now attacks something through the line, or the engine's line uses it. So 6.Be3 no longer “opens a line for your queen”.
  • New facts: a piece left where nothing defends it and taken next move (3.Ng5); “allows checkmate”, naming the pieces that hit the square (f7); “puts a pawn in the centre” (e4 vs a4); “prepares d4” (4.c3); ...Ng4's attack on your bishop.
  • After a lost piece or a mate: only the opponent's reply and your one best answer, as damage control.
  • Wording: no guessing what you “want”; pawns named by the square they stand on; no “worth less”; an attack is only an attack; mate said in words, not “−100.00”.
  • Not built yet: why the better move is better (your point in answers 1 and 2).
