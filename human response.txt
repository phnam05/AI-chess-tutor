Cross-cutting patterns worth writing up separately:

Causal invention (eval attribution) — by far the most common issue, appears in ~13 of 25 cases. The coach routinely explains why an eval is what it is, when Stockfish never gave a reason. Sometimes harmless (small evals, plausible strategic story), sometimes risky (case 8's -4.79 explained by "open files" when the real cause is unknown).

Causal drift — stated purpose of a move doesn't match what the PV actually does (cases 2, 3, 9, 17). Different from invention: the move is real and grounded, but the reason given isn't what the engine line shows.

Two genuine factual errors — case 4 (bishop color) and case 11 (backwards pawn/king logic). These are rarer but more serious than the causal-invention pattern since they're just wrong, not just unverified.

Dataset label/grade mismatches — 4 cases (18, 21, 24, 25) where the case title's quality label contradicts the actual engine grade field. Interestingly, in every one of these the coach text correctly followed the real grade, not the wrong title — so this looks like a metadata/labeling bug in your case file, not a coach failure. Worth checking your labeling pipeline before drawing conclusions from title-based groupings.

Checker under-coverage — a few cases (7, 17, 19) have claims that match the PV correctly but aren't in the grounded field, meaning the checker isn't catching everything it could verify. Not a coach error, but it means "clean (grounded: X)" understates how much of the response is actually checkable.
